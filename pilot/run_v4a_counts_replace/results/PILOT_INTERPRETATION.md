# Pilot results — interpretation and what is safe to put in the proposal

**Run 17/08/2026 · 20 training + 6 sealed compounds × 42 strains × 3 replicates = 4 662 rows ·
all labels simulated (noisy-OR from the pre-registered rule priors).**

---

## 1. What the pilot demonstrates

**Rules are recoverable from structure alone.** Leave-compound-out macro PR-AUC 0.366 against a
prevalence floor that averages 0.04 — a **8.7× lift**, ROC-AUC 0.851. Under the harder
leave-family-out split (whole scaffold family withheld) it drops but survives: PR-AUC 0.229,
**5.8× lift**, ROC-AUC 0.705. The 0.137 PR-AUC gap between the two splits is itself the honest
measure of how much the model leans on scaffold identity rather than on the substructure.

**The flagship rule transfers to sulfate scaffolds it has never seen.** Trained on the two
training sulfates (zosteric acid, apigenin 7-sulfate), the model ranks which strains desulfate
**luteolin 7-sulfate** at ROC-AUC 0.764 and **hesperetin 7-O-sulfate** at 0.767. The second is the
harder case: it is a flavanone, so `flavone_core = 0`, and the prediction rests on the aryl
sulfate substructure alone. This is the single result closest to the proposal's central claim.

**The similarity baseline cannot do this at all.** Tanimoto-kNN assigns one number per compound,
so within a compound it cannot rank strains: **ROC-AUC 0.50 on both sealed sulfates.** The
comparison that matters is not "is the rule model's Brier lower" but "can it say *which strain*
reacts" — and only the rule model can.

**Abstention is calibrated.** On DMSP, which carries no trained substructure, the model puts mean
p(no reaction) = 0.69 and mean max p(any reaction) = 0.195, giving the **lowest Brier of all six
sealed compounds (0.0175)**. The similarity baseline scores 0.0833 on the same compound — 4.8×
worse — because DMSP has zero Tanimoto similarity to everything and the baseline falls back to the
panel average. Predicting correctly that nothing happens is where a rule model beats similarity
most cleanly.

**Marine training beats public/terrestrial training.** A model fitted only on the terrestrial rows
scores Brier 0.1001 on marine substrates against 0.0699 for the marine-trained model —
a **0.0302 advantage**. This is the proposal's secondary success criterion and it holds even here.

---

## 2. What the pilot does **not** demonstrate — and must not be claimed

**The sealed-6 design is underpowered against the similarity baseline.** On the real split
(train 20 → predict 6) the multi-label Brier is 0.0795 for the rule model versus 0.0810 for
Tanimoto-kNN. Bootstrapped over compounds — the honest clustering unit — the difference is
**+0.0014, 95% CI [−0.031, +0.033], P(rule better) = 0.56.** Six compounds cannot resolve this
comparison. The shipped pipeline's own leave-compound-out proxy agrees (P = 0.15) and the
leave-family-out stress test is worse (P = 0.00).

This is not a bug and it is not a reason to change the panel. It is a consequence of two things:
(i) with simulated labels the Tanimoto baseline reads the *same* substructure bits that generated
those labels, so it is an equally-informed competitor rather than a strawman; and (ii) n = 6 is a
small number of clusters. **The aggregate rule-beats-similarity claim has to wait for real LC-MS
labels.** Any Test C win shown on this data would be an artefact.

**Two of the six sealed compounds cannot test what they were chosen to test.** Divanillin's
feature vector is identical to vanillin's, and chicoric acid's to caftaric acid's. The 17-feature
vocabulary has no dimer or ester-count descriptor, so at feature level the model simply reproduces
the training analogue. Chicoric acid is in fact the **worst** of the six (Brier 0.155 vs 0.090 for
similarity) precisely because a confident model is penalised when labels are a fresh stochastic
draw from an identical feature vector.

**Two reaction classes are never exercised.** The sealed set contains no glycoside and no
decarboxylation substrate, so **decarboxylation and deglycosylation have zero positive rows** —
the sealed six test 9 of the 11 classes.

**Test A and Test B numbers should not be quoted.** Test A's JSD of 0.0001 is a machinery
demonstration on labels generated by the very chain being scored. Test B fails on the flagship
desulfation pair (wJaccard 0.028) for a mechanical reason — the shared-probability term is zero
under leave-compound-out — not for a scientific one.

---

## 3. Design consequences worth acting on before OSF registration

1. **The feature vocabulary needs a size/multiplicity descriptor** (number of matches, not
   presence/absence; or an explicit biaryl/dimer flag) — otherwise divanillin and chicoric acid
   are dead weight in the sealed set.
2. **Consider adding a glycoside and a benzoic-acid substrate to the sealed set** so that all
   eleven classes are exercised.
3. **Do not pre-register "rule model beats Tanimoto-kNN on aggregate Brier" as the primary Test C
   endpoint at n = 6.** A within-compound endpoint — can the model rank which strains react —
   is the one this design can actually resolve, and it is the one the baseline cannot answer at all.
4. **Gallic acid's removal cost the `pyrogallol` substructure.** Either accept decarboxylation on
   `benzoic_acid` alone, or restore gallic acid as a 21st training compound.

## 4. Paragraph for insertion into the proposal

> *Preliminary in silico work (WP0).* The rule-learning framework has been implemented and run
> end-to-end on the final compound panel — 20 training compounds, 6 sealed compounds and 42
> rhizosphere strains in triplicate (4 662 substrate × strain observations) — using simulated
> labels generated by noisy-OR from the pre-registered rule priors. This is an engineering
> positive control on rule recovery, not a biological result: no measurement enters it. Under
> leave-compound-out evaluation the framework recovers the generating rule set at a macro PR-AUC
> lift of 8.7× over prevalence (ROC-AUC 0.85), falling to 5.8× under the harder leave-family-out
> split, which quantifies how much of the signal is substructure rather than scaffold. Trained
> only on the two sulfated training compounds, it ranks which strains desulfate the two sealed
> sulfate scaffolds at ROC-AUC 0.76–0.77, including a flavanone that shares no flavone core with
> the training set; the structure-similarity baseline is constant within a compound and therefore
> scores 0.50 on the same task. On DMSP, the sealed compound carrying no trained substructure, the
> framework abstains with mean p(no reaction) = 0.69 and achieves the lowest error of the six.
> The pilot also fixes the evaluation design: with six sealed compounds the aggregate
> rule-versus-similarity comparison is underpowered (ΔBrier 0.001, 95% CI −0.031 to +0.033), so
> the pre-registered Test C endpoint is the within-compound strain ranking that the similarity
> baseline cannot address, and the aggregate comparison is reserved for the experimental atlas.
> What remains to be validated experimentally is everything the simulation assumes: that the rule
> priors reflect real enzymatic preference, that transformations are detectable at the assay
> concentrations, and that strain capability annotations predict realised chemistry.

*(Trim to taste — the load-bearing sentences are the ROC 0.76/0.77-vs-0.50 comparison, the DMSP
abstention, and the explicit statement that the data are simulated.)*
